What the standard adds to a neighbourhood pathway catalogue

The source catalogue already describes coordination across primary care, community services, social care, pharmacy, acute care, virtual wards, carers and neighbourhood MDTs. HPDS adds stable identity, governance, explicit structure, versioning, traceability, semantic binding and a clean separation between the pathway definition and each local implementation.

Operability rather than interoperability alone. In this demonstrator, operability means that a pathway can remain understandable, usable, coordinated, monitorable and safely adaptable across teams, organisations, settings, systems and time. Technical and semantic interoperability are important enabling components, but they do not by themselves demonstrate that the pathway can operate successfully.

Core proposition

The pathway is the stable business artefact. The organisations, systems and interfaces used to realise it can change without changing the identity or clinical/business intent of the pathway.

This is particularly important for neighbourhood care because no single provider or application necessarily owns the end-to-end journey. A catalogue can therefore become the shared reference point for service design, operability, assurance and local variation.

Important limitation

The working catalogue is not yet an HPDS Level 1 catalogue. Entries now record a definition URI, version, lifecycle status (draft), authority status (unapproved) and intended use, and the catalogue records its own version and publisher. Pathway owners, exit points and criteria, principal stages and a catalogue governance authority are still missing.

The demonstrator exposes those gaps rather than filling them silently. Three governance and learning processes are catalogued alongside the care pathways but are not treated as HPDS pathway definitions, because their subject is a service rather than a subject of care.

Single Patient Record lens

The catalogue makes it possible to ask what an SPR must make visible, usable and exchangeable to support pathway operability, while keeping the SPR separate from the pathway definition itself.

HPDS conformance levels

WD 0.3 defines four cumulative conformance levels. A higher level is not inherently better; the appropriate level depends on intended use. Level 1 is marked as the initial target for this catalogue, not as its current state. Entries are assessed only for completeness against the minimum dataset; no conformance is claimed.

Reference definition versus local implementation

The neighbourhood catalogue was deliberately written more cleanly than the earlier acute-EPR catalogues. Local providers, systems, interfaces, deviations and assurance evidence should be attached through PathwayImplementation rather than embedded in the reference definition.
